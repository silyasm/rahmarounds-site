Learn VSEPR

How VSEPR works

VSEPR stands for Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion. Electrons push each other away, so everything around the central atom spreads out as far as it can. That spreading decides the shape.

1Find the central atom

Usually the least electronegative atom, and never hydrogen. Draw the Lewis structure so you can see its bonds and lone pairs.

2Count electron groups

  • Each bonded atom = 1 group
  • Each lone pair on the central atom = 1 group
  • Single, double and triple bonds all count as just 1 group
CO₂ has two double bonds and no lone pairs on C → 2 groups → linear

3Groups give the electron geometry

  • 2 → linear
  • 3 → trigonal planar
  • 4 → tetrahedral
  • 5 → trigonal bipyramidal
  • 6 → octahedral

4Atoms give the molecular shape

Lone pairs still take up space, but you can't "see" them in the shape name. The molecular shape only describes where the atoms are.

H₂O: 2 atoms + 2 lone pairs = 4 groups. Electron geometry is tetrahedral, but the shape is bent.

5Lone pairs push harder

Lone pairs are fatter than bonds, so they squeeze the bond angles smaller. Repulsion order: lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bond > bond–bond.

CH₄ 109.5° → NH₃ ~107° → H₂O ~104.5°

6Where lone pairs sit

  • 5 groups: lone pairs go in the equatorial spots (the flat triangle in the middle), where there's more room
  • 6 groups: the 2nd lone pair goes opposite the first, which is why you get square planar

7AXE shorthand

A = central atom, X = bonded atoms, E = lone pairs.

NH₃ = AX₃E · H₂O = AX₂E₂ · XeF₄ = AX₄E₂

8The big trick

Learn the 5 "parent" shapes. To get the others, swap one atom for a lone pair at a time. Each row below is one family.
